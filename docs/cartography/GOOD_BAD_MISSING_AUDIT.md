## Needs Evidence

1. Every active WID creation, update, version, witness, verification, and export path.
2. Data classification, retention, and deletion behavior across primary and third-party infrastructure.
3. Payment/licensing completion and reconciliation state machines.
4. Creator-domain authentication redirect behavior and deep-link precedence.
5. Public visibility rules for draft, record, testimony, rights, and AI declarations.

## Needs Counsel

1. Ownership, copyrightability, registration, rights transfer, licensing, and enforcement statements.
2. Trademark, collective/certification-mark, publicity/digital-replica, privacy, and contract posture.
3. Geographic scope and applicability of Article 50 and other regulatory requirements.
4. Terms and Privacy consistency with actual hosting, providers, consent, retention, and deletion behavior.

## Needs Adoption

1. Independent creator, partner, and institutional use of WIDs and exports.
2. External verifier trust, signer/key governance, and potentially standards interoperability.
3. Creator use of declaration and consent workflows.
4. Supporter, licensing, and distribution relationships that make the creator economy reciprocal rather than merely hosted.

## Claim-Governance Principle

The FTC’s business guidance says advertising claims must be truthful, not deceptive or unfair, and evidence-based.[1] The audit therefore treats “protected,” “immutable,” “verified,” “permanent,” “AI-safe,” “sovereign,” and earnings/benefit claims as reviewable statements with evidence—not creative copy defaults.
