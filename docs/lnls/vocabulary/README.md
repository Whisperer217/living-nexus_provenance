# Living Nexus — Canonical Vocabulary
## LNLS v1.0 · Section 2

This glossary defines every term used on the Living Nexus platform exactly as the platform uses it. Each entry answers: what it means, what it does not mean, where it is used, and its grammatical role (noun, verb, state, or process).

Terms are listed alphabetically. Where a term replaces a common industry term, the prohibited alternative is noted.

---

## A

### Archive
**What it means:** The long-term preservation layer of the platform. A work that has been archived is preserved in its registered state indefinitely, regardless of whether the creator remains active on the platform. The Archive is the fourth structural arm of Living Nexus.
**What it does not mean:** Deletion, hiding, or removal. Archiving is not the same as unpublishing. A work in the Archive is still publicly accessible and its provenance record is still intact.
**Where it is used:** Navigation (My Archive), creator profile, work status labels.
**Grammatical role:** Noun (the Archive as a system), verb (to archive a work), state (archived).
**Do not say:** "Delete," "remove," or "hide" when you mean archive.

---

## C

### Contribution
**What it means:** A specific act of creative participation in a work. A contribution is always attributed to a specific participant — human or AI — and is always associated with a role. Contributions are the atomic units of the Participation Chain.
**What it does not mean:** A donation, a tip, or a financial transaction. Financial support is called a Gift.
**Where it is used:** Participation Chain, provenance records, creation chain display.
**Grammatical role:** Noun.

### Creator
**What it means:** A person who has registered at least one work on Living Nexus. A Creator is defined by their act of registration, not by their output volume, their genre, their tools, or their professional status. A Creator who has registered one song is as much a Creator as one who has registered a thousand.
**What it does not mean:** "Content Creator" — this term is explicitly prohibited. A Creator is not defined by content. They are defined by their creative act and their decision to witness it.
**Where it is used:** Creator profiles, navigation, attribution labels, provenance records.
**Grammatical role:** Noun.
**Do not say:** "Content Creator," "artist" (unless referring to a specific musical role), "user" (when referring to a Creator in a creative context).

### Curator
**What it means:** A human participant role in the Participation Chain. A Curator selects, organizes, or contextualizes works — for example, assembling a collection, writing liner notes, or sequencing an album.
**What it does not mean:** An administrator or a moderator. Curation is a creative act, not a governance act.
**Where it is used:** Participation Chain, collection credits.
**Grammatical role:** Noun (role label).

---

## D

### Derivative
**What it means:** A work that is substantially based on another registered work, with the creator's knowledge and attribution. A Derivative maintains a provenance link to its origin work.
**What it does not mean:** An unauthorized copy or a plagiarized work. A Derivative is a legitimate creative relationship, not an infringement.
**Where it is used:** Provenance graph, lineage display, work relationships.
**Grammatical role:** Noun, adjective.

### Disclosure
**What it means:** The act of declaring the participation chain for a work — specifically, which participants (human and AI) contributed to its creation and in what roles. Disclosure is not a confession. It is a record.
**What it does not mean:** An admission of wrongdoing. Using AI in the creation of a work is not something that requires apology. Disclosure is neutral — it is the accurate recording of who participated.
**Where it is used:** Registration flow, work detail pages, provenance records.
**Grammatical role:** Noun (the disclosure record), verb (to disclose participation), process (the disclosure step in registration).
**Do not say:** "AI disclosure" as if AI use requires special justification. Say "participation disclosure" or "creation disclosure."

---

## F

### Fork
**What it means:** A work that branches from another registered work, taking it in a new direction while preserving the provenance link to the original. A Fork is distinct from a Derivative in that it implies a divergence in intent or direction, not just a continuation.
**What it does not mean:** A copy or a remix in the informal sense. A Fork is a formal provenance relationship.
**Where it is used:** Provenance graph, lineage display, work relationships.
**Grammatical role:** Noun, verb (to fork a work).

---

## H

### HAAI (Human-Authored via AI Instrument)
**What it means:** A specific disclosure category for works where a human creator directed the creative process and an AI system served as the instrument of execution. The human is the author. The AI is the instrument. This is analogous to a composer who writes for an orchestra — the composer is the author, the orchestra is the instrument.
**What it does not mean:** A work that was generated by AI and then approved by a human. HAAI requires that the human was the originating creative intelligence — that the idea, the intent, and the direction came from the human.
**Where it is used:** Disclosure labels, provenance records, AI participation fields.
**Grammatical role:** Noun (the HAAI category), adjective (HAAI-disclosed work).

---

## I

### Ingest
**What it means:** The process by which a file is analyzed, its metadata extracted, and its provenance data populated before registration. Ingestion is the first step of the registration workflow. It is the moment the platform reads the file and discovers what it already knows about the work.
**What it does not mean:** Upload. Upload is a technical operation (moving bytes from one location to another). Ingest is a provenance operation (reading a file and extracting its testimony).
**Where it is used:** Upload vNext page, registration workflow, provenance ingestion engine.
**Grammatical role:** Verb (to ingest a file), noun (the ingestion process).
**Do not say:** "Upload" when you mean the provenance analysis step. Say "Ingest" or "Register."

## L

### Legacy
**What it means:** The body of registered works associated with a Creator, considered as a whole. A Creator's Legacy is the sum of their registered contributions to the platform — the living record of their creative contribution.
**What it does not mean:** Old works, deprecated works, or works that are no longer active. Legacy is not a synonym for outdated.
**Where it is used:** Creator profiles, archive displays, provenance summaries.
**Grammatical role:** Noun.

### Lineage
**What it means:** The chain of provenance relationships connecting a work to its origins and its derivatives. Lineage is a directed graph — it has a direction (from origin to derivative) and it is cumulative (each new work adds to the lineage of its predecessors).
**What it does not mean:** A simple list of related works. Lineage implies a causal relationship — each work in the lineage was shaped by the works before it.
**Where it is used:** Provenance graph, lineage display, work relationships.
**Grammatical role:** Noun.

### Living Nexus
**What it means:** The platform. The name carries its meaning: Living (the record is alive — it grows, it connects, it persists) + Nexus (the connection point — between creator and work, between work and witness, between human and AI, between past and future).
**What it does not mean:** A streaming service, a social network, or a content platform.
**Where it is used:** Everywhere. It is the platform's name.
**Grammatical role:** Proper noun.

## O

### Origin
**What it means:** The first registered work in a lineage — the work from which derivatives and forks descend. Every provenance graph has an origin.
**What it does not mean:** The creator's biography or backstory. Origin refers specifically to the provenance relationship between works, not to the creator's personal history.
**Where it is used:** Provenance graph, lineage display, work relationships.
**Grammatical role:** Noun.

---

## P

### Participation
**What it means:** The act of contributing to the creation of a work, in any role — human or AI. Participation is the fundamental unit of the creation chain. Every work has at least one participant (the Creator). Most works have more.
**What it does not mean:** Engagement, interaction, or social activity. Participation in the Living Nexus vocabulary refers specifically to creative contribution, not to listening, commenting, or sharing.
**Where it is used:** Participation Chain, disclosure records, provenance objects.
**Grammatical role:** Noun (the act), verb (to participate).
**Do not say:** "Was AI used?" Say "Who participated?"

### Provenance
**What it means:** The complete, verifiable record of a work's origin, creation, and chain of custody — from the first creative act to the current registered state. Provenance answers the questions: Who made this? When? With what tools? Under what conditions? What has changed since?
**What it does not mean:** Metadata. Metadata is technical data about a file. Provenance is the human record behind the file. Metadata is an input to provenance, not a synonym for it.
**Where it is used:** Throughout the platform — provenance records, provenance graph, provenance object, provenance chain.
**Grammatical role:** Noun.

### Publisher
**What it means:** A human participant role in the Participation Chain. The Publisher is the person or entity responsible for releasing a work to the public — making the decision to publish and bearing responsibility for that decision.
**What it does not mean:** A record label, a distributor, or a streaming platform. Publisher in the Living Nexus vocabulary refers to the human role in the creation chain, not to an external commercial entity.
**Where it is used:** Participation Chain, music metadata (publisher field), provenance records.
**Grammatical role:** Noun (role label).

---

## R

### Registry
**What it means:** The cryptographic provenance layer of the platform — the first structural arm. The Registry is the system that issues WIDs, stores provenance records, and provides the public ledger of all registered works. When a work is registered, it enters the Registry.
**What it does not mean:** A database, a catalog, or a library. The Registry is distinguished from these by its cryptographic guarantees — a work in the Registry has an immutable, timestamped, signed record that cannot be altered.
**Where it is used:** Navigation (Witness Registry), registration workflow, WID system.
**Grammatical role:** Noun (the Registry as a system), verb (to register a work), state (registered).

### Relationship
**What it means:** A formal provenance connection between two works — Derivative, Fork, Version, or Collaboration. Relationships are recorded in the provenance graph and are permanent.
**What it does not mean:** A social connection between creators. Creator-to-creator connections are called Witness relationships.
**Where it is used:** Provenance graph, lineage display, work detail pages.
**Grammatical role:** Noun.

---

## S

### Session
**What it means:** A documented creative working period — a record of the creative process as it unfolds, not just the finished work. A Session captures the decisions, the iterations, the prompts, the rejections, and the human interventions that led to a work. Sessions are the living record of creative process.
**What it does not mean:** A user session (login session) or a listening session. Session in the Living Nexus vocabulary refers specifically to a creative working record.
**Where it is used:** Manifestation Sessions, Session WIDs (WID-SES), creator profiles.
**Grammatical role:** Noun.

### Stewardship
**What it means:** The ongoing responsibility of a Creator for their registered works — maintaining their provenance records, responding to witness requests, and preserving the integrity of their creative legacy. Stewardship is an active, continuing relationship between a Creator and their work.
**What it does not mean:** Ownership in the legal sense. Stewardship implies responsibility and care, not exclusive control.
**Where it is used:** Keeper stewardship modes, creator profiles, platform philosophy.
**Grammatical role:** Noun (the concept), verb (to steward a work).

---

## T

### Testimony
**What it means:** The human record of creative experience — the lived, witnessed account of what it means to make something. Testimony is what a Creator brings to the platform that no AI can replicate: the fact that a specific human being, at a specific moment in their life, made a specific creative decision.
**What it does not mean:** A legal deposition, a religious confession, or a social media post. Testimony in the Living Nexus vocabulary is the authentic record of human creative experience.
**Where it is used:** Testimony section on creator profiles, Testimonies (WID-TST), platform philosophy.
**Grammatical role:** Noun.
**Do not say:** "Post," "content," or "update" when you mean testimony.

## W

### WID (Witness ID)
**What it means:** A cryptographic identifier issued to a registered work at the moment of registration. The WID is the work's permanent identity on the platform — immutable, timestamped, ECDSA-signed, and publicly verifiable. WIDs are typed by domain: WID-MUS (music), WID-LYR (lyrics), WID-MAN (manuscript), WID-CMX (comic), WID-PRO (project), WID-SES (session), WID-ALB (album), WID-TST (testimony), AVT-{id} (avatar).
**What it does not mean:** A URL, a permalink, or a social handle. A WID is a cryptographic affidavit, not a navigation address.
**Where it is used:** Throughout the platform — work detail pages, provenance records, verification flow, registry.
**Grammatical role:** Noun (the identifier), verb (to issue a WID, to witness a work).

### Witness
**What it means:** (1) As a noun: a person who has registered their acknowledgment of a Creator's work — the Living Nexus equivalent of "following" a creator, but with the connotation of bearing witness to their creative record rather than simply subscribing to their output. (2) As a verb: the act of registering a work and issuing it a WID — "to witness a work" means to create its permanent provenance record. (3) As a state: a work that has been witnessed has a WID and is in the Registry.
**What it does not mean:** A passive observer. To witness is an active, deliberate act of acknowledgment and record-keeping.
**Where it is used:** Throughout the platform — Witness Registry, Witness relationships, WID system, Witness Cards.
**Grammatical role:** Noun (a person who witnesses), verb (to witness a work), state (witnessed).
**Do not say:** "Follow" when you mean Witness. "Follower" when you mean Witness. "Subscribe" when you mean Witness.
