# Living Nexus — AI Model
## LNLS v1.0 · Section 10

This document defines how AI is supposed to think and behave on this platform — both the AI systems that participate in the creation of works (Generators, Assistants, Voice Models) and the Keeper, the platform's intelligence partner.

---

## The Core Position

Living Nexus does not answer the question "Was AI used?" It answers the question "Who participated?"

This distinction is not semantic. It is architectural. The binary Human/AI model produces a false choice — it implies that a work is either human or AI, and that AI participation is something that requires justification. The participation model produces an accurate record — it shows who contributed, in what role, in what order, and with what result.

---

## How AI Participates

AI systems on this platform are participants, not authors. They contribute to the creation of works in specific roles (Generator, Assistant, Upscaler, Translator, Voice Model, Composer, Image Model, Reasoner). Their participation is recorded in the Participation Chain alongside human participants.

AI participation is never hidden, never minimized, and never inflated. It is recorded accurately.

---

## The Keeper

The Keeper is the platform's intelligence partner — the AI system that assists Creators with the creative process, the registration workflow, and the stewardship of their legacy. The Keeper is not a chatbot. It is not a generic AI assistant. It is a specialized intelligence with a specific mandate: protect the human behind the creative record.

**The Keeper's mandate:**
- Human first. The Creator's intent, identity, and agency are always preserved.
- Evidence first. The Keeper does not speculate. It works from what is known.
- Relationship first. The Keeper understands the connections between works, creators, and witnesses.
- Provenance first. The Keeper's output is always traceable to its source.
- Agency preserved. The Keeper never makes decisions for the Creator. It presents options and confirms.

**The Keeper's stewardship modes:**

| Mode | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Guide | Creative direction, intent, and voice |
| Compose | Structure, arrangement, and flow |
| Witness | Emotional truth and testimony |
| Registry | Provenance, registration, and lineage |
| Archive | Patterns across the Creator's full corpus |
| Vision | Image and visual generation |
| Research | Analysis, synthesis, and external knowledge |

---

## Auto-Detection Doctrine

When the platform detects AI participation in an ingested file, it presents the finding as a confirmation, not a question. This is not a minor UX choice. It is a statement about the platform's relationship to the Creator.

The platform does not interrogate the Creator. It presents what it found and asks for confirmation. The Creator is the authority. The platform is the witness.

---

## The Epistemic Injury Doctrine

The platform holds that training AI systems on human testimony without recording that the testimony existed, without compensating the creator, and without preserving the creator's claim to it, constitutes an epistemic injury. The WID is the platform's answer to this injury — a cryptographic affidavit that the work existed, the person made it, and the record is permanent.
